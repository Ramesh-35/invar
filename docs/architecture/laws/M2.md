# M2 — The Law of Structural Evolution

> A codebase is not static. INVAR's invariants must survive, and detect,
> structural change over time — regime shifts, not just point defects.

## Statement

M2 asserts that the failures worth owning are often **structural breaks**: the
moment a system's shape changes (a schema is split, a contract is dropped, a
clock source is swapped) such that a previously-true invariant silently becomes
false. TC-001 is the archetype — the break is the producer's refactor, and the
damage is realised later, elsewhere. The bi-temporal ledger exists so that such
breaks are recorded against both when they became true (`t_v`) and when they were
observed (`t_r`), making the shift itself a first-class, queryable object.

## Detection stance

- `[HYPOTHESIS]` Structural breaks in source/AST history can be detected as
  change-points over a suitable signal derived from the lineage ledger.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` That a **robust CUSUM** (or the CBR-001 market-regime detector)
  is a *proven* method for source-code structural breaks. CUSUM is a
  change-point technique from a different domain; its transfer to ASTs is a
  hypothesis to be tested, not an established result (see DR-006). `invar-cusum`
  is `[DEFERRED]` precisely because this has not been earned.

## What M2 requires before any detector is written

1. A precise definition of the signal a break is detected *on*.
2. A labelled set of real structural breaks to test a detector against.
3. A demonstration that the detector's true/false-positive behaviour is
   measured, not assumed.

Until those exist, M2 is a **design commitment**, not a working capability.
