# INVAR logo brief: the hawk (saved 2026-10-11, design when called)

Founder direction: a hawk, inspired by two reference marks he supplied (a
navy eagle with gold accents clutching a gold arrow, dynamic wings-up pose;
a brown hawk mid-strike). The references set the QUALITY bar, not the
content: the final mark must be fully original, no element copied or
recognizably derived from them or from any existing logo, and it must not
read as any known brand's bird. INVAR's own IP rules apply to its logo.

## What the hawk means here (the idea the mark must carry)

A hawk is the right animal for the right reason: visual acuity. It sees the
small break at distance that everything else walks past. INVAR's whole
character is in two lines: it sees what changed, and it says what it could
not see. So:

- The hawk WATCHES; it does not strike. No talons-out attack pose, no prey,
  no arrow, no lightning bolt. INVAR evaluates and states; it never mutates
  code. A perched or banking hawk, head keen, reads "sentinel", which is
  the truthful posture. (This is the deliberate break from both references,
  which are attack poses.)
- The eye is the focal point. One sharp geometric eye, the smallest and
  highest-contrast element, placed on a golden-ratio point of the
  composition. If the mark says one thing, it says "seen".

## Concept directions (explore in this order, pick by the tests below)

1. The sentinel: hawk in profile, perched, body a single continuous
   stroke-free silhouette; between the folded wing and the body, a
   negative-space vertical bar reading as the I of INVAR (or as the
   "contract line" between two repositories: producer one side, consumer
   the other, the hawk watching the boundary).
2. The bracket hawk: wings raised in a calm V (not combat), the
   negative space between wings and body forming a subtle checkmark-free
   V of INVAR; silhouette must read as a bird first, letter second.
3. The circle hawk: head-only mark, hawk head in profile inscribed in
   golden-ratio circles (beak curve, skull curve, eye position all from
   phi-related radii), monoline or solid. Strongest at favicon size.

## Construction (non-negotiable)

- Golden ratio grid: a documented construction of circles/rectangles with
  radii in phi progression (r, r*1.618, r*2.618...); every major curve
  (skull, beak, chest, wing shoulder) snaps to a construction circle; the
  eye sits at an intersection of golden sections. Ship the construction
  overlay as its own SVG layer so the discipline is inspectable, the way
  the best geometric marks publish their grids.
- Flat, solid shapes only. No gradients, no outlines thinner than 1/32 of
  the mark height, no detail that dies at 16 px.
- Colors, two, from the founder's reference palette: deep navy
  (#1E2A44-family) as the body, a restrained gold (#F2B300-family, final
  hex at design time) ONLY for the eye or one accent, never for half the
  mark. Must work in pure monochrome first; color is applied, not relied on.

## The tests (a candidate fails any one, it dies)

1. 16 px favicon: still unmistakably a hawk, eye still visible.
2. One color, one second: silhouette alone identifiable with no internal
   detail.
3. Terminal test: reads correctly as a dark mark on light and light on
   dark without redrawing.
4. Trace test: put it beside the two references and any well-known bird
   mark; a stranger must NOT say "that's based on X".
5. Honesty test: posture is watching, not attacking.
6. Lockup: works standalone and beside the wordmark INVAR (geometric sans,
   all caps, tracking wide; the wordmark never touches the bird; spacing
   unit = the eye diameter, phi-multiplied).

## Deliverables when built

Master SVG (mark), mono light/dark SVGs, construction-grid SVG overlay,
favicon 16/32, GitHub social preview 1280x640 (mark + wordmark + one line:
"Cross-repository contracts, stated honestly"), and a one-page USAGE.md
(clear space, minimum sizes, the two colors, what never to do).

References: two images supplied by the founder in chat on 2026-10-11,
described above; deliberately not committed to the repository (third-party
designs; qualities studied, elements never copied).
